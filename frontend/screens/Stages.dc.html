The wake-up ladder

Swipe through the four stages. Each one only starts if the last wasn't enough.

Stage 1

Blue light

When you're a bit drowsy

What it does

A dim blue glow at the edge of your view, held for 30 minutes.

Why we chose it

Blue light suppresses melatonin, the sleep hormone. That takes tens of minutes, so it stays on quietly in the background instead of flashing.

Stage 2

Mint mist

When drowsiness keeps building

What it does

A 10-second burst of peppermint mist.

Why we chose it

Peppermint wakes you through a different sense, fast, and it doesn't fade the way a repeated sound does. It waits 2 minutes between bursts because the nose adapts quickly.

Stage 3

Chime and voice

When you're very drowsy

What it does

A slow chime, then Ollie says: “Your eyes are closing. Let’s find a place to rest.”

Why we chose it

A calm spoken warning you hear and understand, with a clear next step: take a break.

Stage 4

Rising alarm

Extreme drowsiness, or any microsleep

What it does

Fast beeps, three a second, growing 30 dB louder, with Ollie’s voice and a long burst of mint.

Why we chose it

It has to wake you, but a sudden blast can startle a driver into swerving. So it rises instead of starting at full volume.
